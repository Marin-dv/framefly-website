The short version. Keep it under 90 seconds for a landing page or a launch, and put the point in the first half. Record it yourself if you have a day and want control. Hire it out if the video is the centre of a campaign and you have thousands to spend. Use an interactive demo when people should click, not watch. Whichever way, write the script before you record anything.

How long it should be

Wistia's 2026 State of Video report looked at more than 13 million videos hosted on its platform. Videos under one minute averaged a 52% engagement rate, and product videos under a minute averaged 50%. In plain terms, the typical viewer leaves around the halfway mark.

Two things follow. A short video is watched in a larger share, so cut everything that is not the point. And whatever the length, the claim and the proof belong in the first half, because half your viewers will not see the second.

Wistia places product demos in the one to five minute range, and longer walkthroughs between five and thirty minutes. So as a rule of thumb:

  • Launch or landing page video: 30 to 90 seconds.
  • Product demo for someone already interested: one to five minutes.
  • Onboarding walkthrough: as long as the task takes, in chapters.

1. Record it yourself

Open a screen recorder, walk through the product, edit. Recorders such as Screen Studio, Loom or Tella take care of part of the polish. The software is cheap. The cost is your time: a script, several takes because something always loads late, then cutting, captions and an export for each platform. For a first video, plan on a day. That is our estimate, not a measured figure.

2. Hire a freelancer or an agency

ContentBeta, a video agency for software companies, publishes a 2026 buyer guide with three bands per finished minute: $2,000 to $5,000 for a freelancer or basic production, $6,000 to $15,000 for a good SaaS video agency, and $18,000 to $40,000 or more for a premium US agency. The middle band usually covers the script, interface animation, voice-over, music and two to four rounds of changes.

Read those numbers for what they are: the guide says they reflect what its own clients reported on discovery calls, not an audited survey, and it comes from a company that sells this service. They are still the clearest published ranges we found.

3. Build an interactive demo

An interactive demo is a click-through copy of your product that lives on your site. It is not a video: you cannot upload it to YouTube or put it in a launch gallery. But the data on what keeps people in one is useful for videos too.

Arcade's 2026 benchmarks cover 14 million demo sessions. Demos of 9 to 12 steps were completed 40% of the time, against 22% for demos of one to five steps. Step 7 is where most people leave, and those who pass it are 2.3 times more likely to finish. Demos with an AI voice-over were completed 14% more often. These are one vendor's numbers from its own platform, so take them as a direction, not a law.

The steps that apply to all four

  1. Write the script first. One viewer, one problem, one thing they should do at the end. If you cannot say it in two sentences, the video is not ready to be filmed.
  2. Open on the result. Show the finished thing in the first seconds, then how you got there. Half the audience is gone by the midpoint.
  3. Use a demo account with believable data. An empty dashboard films badly, and your real one shows customers.
  4. Let the voice set the timing. Record or generate the narration before you cut. When we measured synthesized narration for our own plans it ran at about 190 words a minute, not the 150 we had assumed, so every section was shorter than planned.
  5. Add captions. They are the script, word for word, and they let the video work with the sound off.
  6. Export a shape for each place. 16:9 for your site and YouTube, 9:16 for short-form feeds, 1:1 or 4:5 for the rest.

Common questions

How long should a SaaS demo video be?

For a landing page or a launch, 30 to 90 seconds. Wistia's 2026 data on more than 13 million videos shows videos under a minute average a 52% engagement rate, and viewers tend to leave around halfway. A demo for someone already evaluating the product can run one to five minutes.

How much does a product demo video cost?

Doing it yourself costs a recorder and about a day. A freelancer runs $2,000 to $5,000 per finished minute and a good SaaS video agency $6,000 to $15,000, according to ContentBeta's 2026 buyer guide, which is based on what its clients reported. AI tools that film the app charge from nothing to tens of dollars a video.

Do I need a voice-over?

It helps people stay. In Arcade's 2026 benchmarks, interactive demos with an AI voice-over were completed 14% more often than those without. That figure is for interactive demos, not videos, but the reason carries over: a voice tells the viewer where to look.

What is the difference between a demo video and an interactive demo?

A demo video is a file people watch, and you can post it anywhere a video goes. An interactive demo is a click-through copy of the product on a web page. Use the video to explain, and the interactive demo to let a visitor try.

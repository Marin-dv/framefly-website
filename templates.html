Twenty ways to set the words.

Captions are the script, word for word, timed on the voice. Type a sentence of your own and watch each style say it.

PopTwo or three heavy words, the spoken one grows and takes the colour.
KaraokeA full line in outlined type, each word lighting up as it is said.
BlocksEvery word in its own box, the spoken one in colour.
One wordA single huge word at a time. For hooks and very short clips.
MarkerBold type with a highlighter stroke moving from word to word.
PunchSlanted capitals on a hard shadow, like a poster.
StackOne word per line, stacked, the spoken line in colour.
OutlineHollow letters that fill in as the voice reaches them.
NeonWhite type, the spoken word glowing.
LabelA strip of white tape, slightly askew, the spoken word marked.
BubbleA message bubble, the spoken word in a dark pill.
TypewriterTyped out word by word behind a caret.
SubtitleWhite on a dark bar: readable on anything.
CinemaWarm film subtitles with a soft shadow and no bar.
Closed captionMonospaced white on solid black, as on television.
Lower thirdA panel set to the left with a coloured edge, like a news strap.
CleanPlain white type, the words still to come held back.
Fade inEach word appears as it is said and stays.
UnderlinePlain type with a coloured rule under the spoken word.
GlassA frosted pill that lets the picture through.
